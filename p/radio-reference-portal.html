// Marine VHF · USA & Hawaii

Marine VHF Channel Reference

Most commonly monitored marine radio frequencies for recreational boaters, commercial traffic, harbor operations, Coast Guard, and NOAA weather in US and Hawaii waters.

📡

Always Monitor CH 16

Channel 16 (156.800 MHz) is the international distress and calling frequency. All VHF-equipped vessels must monitor it at all times.

🌊

Working Channels

Establish contact on a hailing channel, then move to a working channel. Keeps hailing channels clear for new contacts and emergencies.

⚡

Hawaii Notes

CH 14 (156.700 MHz) is actively used by Honolulu VTS. WX1 (162.550 MHz) is typically the clearest NOAA signal in the Oahu region.

// Amateur Radio · HAM

HAM Radio Frequencies

Key amateur radio frequencies across HF, VHF, and UHF bands. Requires an FCC amateur radio license to transmit. Anyone may listen.

📻

Licensing

Three license classes: Technician, General, and Amateur Extra. Each grants progressively more operating privileges across the spectrum.

🔁

Repeaters

VHF/UHF repeaters extend range dramatically. Most use a tone (CTCSS/PL) to access. Hawaii has an active repeater network across islands.

🌍

HF Propagation

HF bands (3–30 MHz) can travel thousands of miles via ionospheric skip. Band conditions vary with solar activity and time of day.

// Satellite & Space Communications

Satellite Frequencies

Common satellite and space-related frequencies that can often be monitored using consumer radios and SDR receivers — no special license required to listen.

📡

SDR Recommended

A $25 RTL-SDR dongle is all you need to receive most of these signals. Use SDR# or GQRX on a laptop or Raspberry Pi.

🌍

APT Weather Images

NOAA weather satellites broadcast analog images at 137 MHz. With an SDR and a V-dipole antenna you can decode live satellite photos.

🚀

Doppler Shift

Fast-moving satellites cause frequency shift as they approach and pass. Software like Gpredict can compensate automatically during tracking.

// Aviation & Airband

Aviation Frequencies

Civil and military aviation uses AM modulation on 118–136.975 MHz. Monitoring only — a license is required to transmit on aviation frequencies.

🛫

AM Modulation

Aviation uses Amplitude Modulation (AM), not FM. Make sure your receiver supports AM for the airband — most SDRs and dedicated scanners do.

🗼

Honolulu Approach

Honolulu International uses 126.9 MHz for approach. Tower operations vary — a scanner with frequency search is the best way to find active channels locally.

⚠

Guard Frequency

121.500 MHz is the international emergency "Guard" frequency. All military aircraft monitor it continuously. Also used for ELT beacon detection.

// NOAA Weather Radio

NOAA Weather Broadcasts

NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards broadcasts continuous weather forecasts, warnings, and emergency alerts 24/7. Passive receive — no license required.

🌦

WX1 — Oahu Primary

162.550 MHz (WX1) is typically the clearest NOAA signal in the Oahu/Honolulu region. Always the first channel to check in Hawaii waters.

🔔

SAME Alerts

NOAA uses SAME (Specific Area Message Encoding) to broadcast targeted warnings. Weather radios with SAME can filter alerts for specific counties.

📻

All Hazards

Not just weather — NOAA WR also broadcasts AMBER alerts, Presidential alerts, and other emergency notifications across the US.

Emergency Channels

Key emergency, safety, and public service frequencies. Always monitor — never transmit on emergency channels unless in genuine distress.

// Digital Modes & Data Signals

Digital Radio Modes

Digital signals sound like noise to the human ear but carry structured data. Many can be decoded with free software and an SDR or audio cable from a radio.

📶

FT8 — Weak Signal

FT8 can decode signals 20 dB below the noise floor. Wildly popular on HF; thousands of stations active globally at any given moment.

📍

APRS — Position Data

APRS carries GPS positions, weather data, and messages over packet radio. 144.390 MHz is the North American APRS frequency.

🚢

AIS — Vessel ID

AIS broadcasts ship identity, position, course, and speed. Receivable with any SDR — free tools like OpenCPN can display live vessel traffic.

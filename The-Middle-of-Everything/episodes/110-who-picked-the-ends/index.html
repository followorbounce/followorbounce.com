Who Picked the Ends?

One true sentence, one minute of looking, and an answer to the first day's question.

Finale, part two. Chris's eyes are open. Nobody's home. To bring them back, someone has to say one true sentence about them. Specific.

On the Observatory hill before dawn, everyone who loves Chris tries to find the right sentence, and the person who is good at measurements, not sentences, tries measuring. At six o'clock the Label becomes final, so Halfway gets one unusual broadcast: go and look at one thing, closely, for one minute. Then Ori finally answers a question from the first day.

What's real: the “middle” is a midpoint on a powers-of-ten ruler. From the Planck length to the observable universe it's about 0.12 mm; start at a proton and it's about 1,000 km; start at an atom and it's most of the way to the Moon. The ends are choices, not laws. Drift and Tarrow's sentence are the show's inventions.

Did you spot Ninety-One? It's hiding somewhere in this episode.
